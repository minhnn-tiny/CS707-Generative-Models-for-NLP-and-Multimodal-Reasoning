\begin{abstract}
Sketches let a person tell a robot \emph{which} object to manipulate (a circle) and \emph{where} it should go (an arrow) without naming anything, but existing sketch-conditioned robot systems assume the drawing is correct. We propose \textbf{sketch-prompt validation}---deciding \emph{before} any action whether a drawn instruction is correct, consistent across circle, arrow, and caption, and sufficient for a unique action, and saying why not---as an explicit, benchmarked task. A pilot simulator-grounded generator over LIBERO yields 37,970 labelled prompts; frontier vision-language models (VLMs) fail it zero-shot (best 0.79 accuracy), a 500M LoRA-tuned validator reaches 0.90, yet a geometry-only classifier reaches 0.93, exposing shortcuts. We propose to (H1) confirm learnability against strong baselines, (H2) remove shortcuts with a distribution-matched, adversarially filtered generator audited by scene-blind ceilings, (H3) \emph{generate} a per-predicate verdict and rejection reason behind a conformal false-accept guarantee, and (H4) test transfer to human sketches.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem and Motivation}
\label{sec:problem}

Language is ambiguous in cluttered scenes with duplicate objects and goal images are over-specified \citep{sundaresan2024rtsketch}; a circle-and-arrow sketch with a name-free caption (``move this there'') is a persistent deictic act \citep{sauppe2014deictics,kranstedt2006deixis}. Recent systems condition robot policies on such sketches \citep{sundaresan2024rtsketch,gu2024rttrajectory,li2025crayonrobo,li2025rovi} and all silently assume the drawing is \emph{valid}. Yet circles miss or enclose a distractor, arrows point to the wrong container or detach from the circle, captions contradict sketches, and channels that look fine alone can jointly disagree; executing such a sketch commits the robot to the wrong action.

\begin{figure}[b]
\centering

\caption{The pilot validator as a pre-execution trust gate \citep{drawvla2026pilot}: a LoRA-adapted VLM trained on simulator ground truth judges the marked image and caption; \accept{} executes, \reject{} asks the user to redraw.}
\label{fig:overview}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Problem (Track B).} \emph{Can a model decide, before any action, whether a drawn instruction is safe to execute---and say why not?} We formulate \textbf{sketch-prompt validation} as a decision over image, circle, arrow, and caption requiring per-channel correctness, cross-channel consistency, and sufficiency (\Secref{sec:formulation}), plus a generated reason; no existing task captures it (\Secref{sec:related}). We address it with generative VLMs as zero-shot judges and as LoRA-tuned validators (\Figref{fig:overview}).

\textbf{Pilot evidence and gap.} A pilot by team members \citep{drawvla2026pilot} built \dvv, a simulator-grounded generator over LIBERO \citep{liu2023libero} producing 37,970 labelled prompts across 40 tasks with no manual annotation. Frontier VLMs fail zero-shot (best 0.79 accuracy, 0.43 MCC; majority class 0.74) while SmolVLM-500M \citep{marafioti2025smolvlm} with LoRA \citep{hu2022lora} reaches 0.90 (Table~\ref{tab:pilot}, Appendix~\ref{app:details}); but a geometry-only classifier reaches 0.93, i.e., the generator leaks shortcuts \citep{geirhos2020shortcut}, and the pilot was single-seed with one zero-shot prompt and no ablations, human sketches, or explanations. This project delivers those deltas.

\textbf{Hypotheses.} \textbf{H1 (learnability):} a $\le$1B LoRA-tuned VLM beats the best zero-shot VLM under its best prompt by $\ge$10 accuracy points and is within 1 balanced-accuracy point of, or exceeds, a fine-tuned discriminative model of similar size, over 3 seeds. \textbf{H2 (shortcut removal):} on the hardened tier the multimodal validator keeps $\ge$0.80 balanced accuracy and exceeds both scene-blind ceilings (geometry-only and blank-canvas, on the untouched audit split) by $\ge$15 points. \textbf{H3 (structured supervision):} supervising the five validity predicates as auxiliary generative targets improves the directional and grounding slices (the pilot's weakest) by $\ge$3 points over binary-only training, and generated reasons name the violated predicate with $\ge$80\% faithfulness. \textbf{H4 (sim-to-human transfer):} validators trained with guarded imprecision augmentation lose $\le$10 balanced-accuracy points on human sketches; removing it widens the gap by $\ge$5 points. \textbf{Gate:} empirical test FAR at $\hat\tau$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:crc}) is $\le0.05$ in every seed on v1 and v2.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Sketch- and visual-prompt-based instruction.} RT-Sketch and RT-Trajectory condition policies on goal or trajectory sketches \citep{sundaresan2024rtsketch,gu2024rttrajectory}, RoVI decodes an arrow/circle language with a VLM \citep{li2025rovi}, CrayonRobo \emph{tolerates} noisy prompts but does not classify them \citep{li2025crayonrobo}, Action-Sketcher and AnyUser generate or translate sketches \citep{tan2026actionsketcher,yang2026anyuser}, and visual-prompting methods \citep{nasiriany2024pivot,yang2023som,liu2024moka,yuan2024robopoint,cai2024vipllava} annotate images so a VLM can ground spatial decisions. All assume the prompt is valid; \emph{we extend} them by making validity the primary decision.

\textbf{Verification and safety gating.} KnowNo asks for help via conformal prediction \citep{ren2023knowno}; VerifyLLM and VeriGraph verify plans against temporal logic or scene graphs \citep{sautenkov2025verifyllm,ekpo2024verigraph}; DoReMi and REFLECT detect or explain execution failures \citep{guo2024doremi,liu2023reflect}. These verify \emph{plans} or \emph{executions}; \emph{we contrast} by verifying the instruction itself, an earlier gate, with a conformal threshold \citep{angelopoulos2024conformalrisk,geifman2017selective}. \textbf{Shortcuts.} A geometry-only classifier is a hypothesis-only baseline in the NLI sense \citep{gururangan2018artifacts}; AFLite \citep{lebras2020aflite} and dataset cartography \citep{swayamdipta2020cartography} remove or expose artefacts \citep{geirhos2020shortcut} in fixed datasets; \emph{we control the generator}.

\section{Task Formulation and Method Sketch}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Formulation}
\label{sec:formulation}
A sketch prompt is $P=(I,C,A,L)$: RGB observation $I$, circle $C$ (centre, radius) denoting \emph{which} object, arrow $A$ (tail, head) denoting \emph{where} it goes, and a name-free caption $L$. From simulator ground truth we define predicates $v_C$ (the circle uniquely and correctly selects the target), $v_A$ (correct bearing and magnitude, anchored on the circled object, ending in the intended region), $v_L$ (caption verb and implicit referents match the intended action), cross-modal consistency $v_X$ (all channels describe the same object, destination, and predicate), and sufficiency $v_S$ (the prompt determines a unique executable action). A prompt is valid iff $V(P)=v_C(P)\wedge v_A(P)\wedge v_L(P)\wedge v_X(P)\wedge v_S(P)$; the task is to learn $f_\theta(P)\in\{\accept,\reject\}$ predicting $V(P)$, with a reason on \reject. A prompt can pass every per-channel predicate yet be invalid because $v_X=0$ or $v_S=0$, so validation is not a set of independent checks.

\subsection{Simulator-grounded data generation and shortcut hardening}
\label{sec:data}
\textbf{Pilot pipeline (\Figref{fig:overview}; Appendix \Figref{fig:method}, top).} From LIBERO's object poses, region geometry, and gripper trajectories we extract the correct circle target and arrow endpoints for every frame with no human labelling. Valid prompts get \emph{guarded} human-imprecision augmentation (jitter, non-circular strokes, hand-like shafts): an in-point guard keeps the target inside the circle, an out-point guard keeps distractors outside. Negatives apply one controlled corruption from four families (14 of 18 modes implemented; Appendix~\ref{app:taxonomy}) plus five grounding configurations, so every negative has a known mode, severity, and violated predicate.

\textbf{New: shortcut hardening (H2).} Let $g(P)$ be the raw geometry vector (circle centre and radius, arrow endpoints, length, bearing). (i)~\emph{Distribution matching}: negatives draw the same imprecision magnitudes as positives on the valid side of a guard, and corruptions that must cross a guard (near-miss, wrong magnitude) draw severities from the mirrored distribution just beyond it, so class-conditional marginals of $g$ overlap as far as labels allow. (ii)~\emph{Adversarial filtering} \citep{lebras2020aflite} on medium/hard modes only: we repeatedly train geometry-only gradient-boosted classifiers on random folds, score each example by how often held-out classifiers predict it confidently ($>0.75$), and remove the most predictable until geometry-only balanced accuracy on an \emph{untouched} audit split (also probed with MLP and $k$-NN) is $\le0.60$ or retention falls below 60\%. Since pixel artefacts can leak even when coordinates do not, we also report a \emph{scene-blind} ceiling (sketches on a blank canvas).

\subsection{Structured validator and conformal inference}
\label{sec:validator}
\textbf{Model.} A compact VLM (SmolVLM-256M/500M) with LoRA takes the image with the rendered sketch, the caption, and a fixed prompt and generates a \emph{structured verdict}: five predicate questions $q_k$, $k\in\{C,A,L,X,S\}$, answered sequentially with earlier answers in context (teacher-forced in training), then the \accept/\reject{} question $q_V$, then, on \reject, a one-sentence reason. Predicate labels are free (each negative violates a known subset; positives satisfy all); we also report the derived decision $\hat V'=\bigwedge_k\hat v_k$ and its disagreement with $\hat V$.

\textbf{Objective.} Each yes/no answer is scored by forced choice over its two answer tokens, $p_\theta(\text{yes}\mid P,q_k)=\sigma(z_{\text{yes}}-z_{\text{no}})$; the reason uses token-level cross-entropy $\ell_R$ on \reject{} examples against one of $\ge$3 paraphrased template reasons per mode (one held out). We minimise
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}(\theta)=\ell_V(\theta)+\lambda\!\!\sum_{k\in\{C,A,L,X,S\}}\!\!\ell_k(\theta)+\mu\,\ell_R(\theta),\qquad
\ell_k(\theta)=-\log p_\theta\big(y_k \mid P, q_k\big),
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
with $\lambda,\mu$ tuned on validation; $\lambda=\mu=0$ recovers the pilot's binary-only validator (ablation A1).

\textbf{Inference with a finite-sample false-accept guarantee.} Let $s(P)=p_\theta(\accept\mid P,q_V)$; we \accept{} iff $s(P)\ge\hat\tau$. False accepts (executing an invalid prompt) are the safety-critical error, so we bound the false-accept rate (FAR) by conformal risk control \citep{angelopoulos2024conformalrisk}: on the $n$ \emph{invalid} validation prompts let $\ell_i(\tau)=\mathbb{1}[s(P_i)\ge\tau]$ and
\begin{equation}
\hat\tau=\inf\big\{\tau:\ \tfrac{1}{n+1}\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ell_i(\tau)+\tfrac{1}{n+1}\le\alpha\big\}.
\label{eq:crc}
\end{equation}
If invalid validation and test prompts are exchangeable, $\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{FAR}(\hat\tau)]\le\alpha$ ($\alpha=0.05$); we report the induced false-reject rate and empirical coverage across seeds, tiers, and the human set (where exchangeability is \emph{not} guaranteed); rejected prompts return the reason.

\textbf{Datasets and licences.} (1)~\emph{LIBERO} \citep{liu2023libero} (MIT): 800 demonstrations, 40 tasks. (2)~\emph{\dvv{} v1} (pilot): 37,970 examples---30,302 train / 3,702 val / 3,966 test, demo-disjoint; 9,098 positives, 28,872 negatives (Appendix~\ref{app:taxonomy}). (3)~\emph{v2 (hardened)}: same splits after matching and filtering ($\ge$60\% retention expected) plus the untouched audit split. (4)~\emph{Human sketch test set} (evaluation only; cf.\ \Figref{fig:evalsets}; SMU IRB exemption sought first): $\approx$10 adult volunteers each draw circle+arrow prompts on 30 demo-disjoint frames, 15 \emph{unprompted} and 15 \emph{deliberately wrong} ($\approx$300 sketches; no captions, so $v_L$/$v_X$ are out of scope); guards give labels, two members annotate blind. Data and adapters: CC~BY~4.0; code: MIT.

\textbf{Metrics.} Accuracy, balanced accuracy, macro-F1, MCC (74\% negative test set); FAR and FRR at $\hat\tau$ and FAR at FRR\,=\,10\%; per-family and per-mode slice accuracy; geometry-only and scene-blind ceilings per tier; the transfer gap $\Delta_{\text{human}}$ (balanced accuracy); reason faithfulness on true rejects against the ground-truth predicate and the model's own $\hat v$ (string match and a Qwen3-8B judge); a 5-point usefulness rating of 50 reasons.


\textbf{Baselines.} (i)~Majority class. (ii)~Zero-shot open VLMs Qwen3-VL-2B/4B/8B \citep{qwen3vl2025} and (iii)~closed APIs (Gemini 3.7 Flash, Claude Haiku 4.5/Sonnet 4.6), pinned and cached; per model the best of direct, chain-of-thought, 4-shot, and coordinates-as-text prompts is chosen on a 500-example validation subset. (iv)~Geometry-only gradient boosting \citep{ke2017lightgbm} and the scene-blind validator as ceilings. (v)~A fine-tuned discriminative model of similar size (ConvNeXt-T with late fusion of $g(P)$ and a caption embedding). (vi)~The pilot's binary-only SmolVLM+LoRA. \emph{Ours:} structured SmolVLM+LoRA with the conformal gate.

\textbf{Ablations ($\ge$3), sensitivity, statistics.} A1: structured (+reason) vs.\ binary-only ($\lambda=\mu=0$; H3). A2: train on v2 vs.\ v1, test on both tiers (H2). A3: modality (no caption; coordinates-as-text; scene-blind). A4: no guarded-imprecision augmentation (H4). A5: prompt-only vs.\ LoRA. A6: backbone 256M/500M/2.2B. Sensitivity: training-set size, LoRA rank, $\tau$ sweep, corruption severity. All trained models use 3 seeds (mean$\pm$std); McNemar's test \citep{dietterich1998tests} on seed-matched predictions, paired-bootstrap 95\% CIs, minimum detectable effects per slice.

\section{Risks, Resources, and Team Plan}
\label{sec:risks}
\textbf{Risks and mitigations.} \textbf{Hardening over-removes or still leaks:} per-mode retention curves, removal capped at 40\%, both tiers released. \textbf{Small human set; guard label noise:} evaluation-only probe with bootstrap CIs; blind audit of 200 positives and 200 negatives, flagging modes with agreement $<0.8$. \textbf{API drift, cost, compute:} pinned versions, cached pilot outputs, Qwen3-VL primary, ablations on the 256M model; $\approx$160 GPU-h on one A100-40GB/RTX 4090 plus $\approx$USD~100--150 of API calls (reproducibility statement). \textbf{Schedule:} go/no-go 25 Oct---if the v2 ceiling is still $\ge0.75$ at 60\% retention we report v1 plus the best partial tier and shift effort to H3/H4. \textbf{Over-claiming safety:} the gate reduces, not eliminates, unsafe executions; no closed-loop control is evaluated.

\textbf{Roles and timeline (2026).} \emph{Member~1 (data):} generator v2, hardening, audit, release. \emph{Member~2 (modelling):} structured objective, reasons, gate, LoRA training, ablations. \emph{Member~3 (evaluation):} API baselines, statistics, human study, ethics. \emph{1--11 Oct:} IRB, recruitment. \emph{12--25 Oct:} generator v2, 3-seed re-run with stronger baselines (H1), validator and gate, A1--A3; go/no-go. \emph{26 Oct--8 Nov:} human study (H4), A4--A6, sensitivity, statistics. \emph{9--12 Nov:} final presentation ($\ge$90\% of results); \emph{13--27 Nov:} final report and artifacts.

\subsection*{Ethics statement}
All training data are simulator renders of tabletop scenes with no people or personal information. The human sketch study involves adult volunteers who give informed consent; only drawings and anonymous ratings are stored. The validator is a trust gate for robot instructions: a false accept could cause an incorrect physical action, so we report false-accept rates with confidence intervals and recommend deployment with human confirmation. We comply with the MIT licence of LIBERO, the Apache-2.0 licences of SmolVLM and Qwen3-VL, and the terms of service of the closed APIs, and release our data under CC~BY~4.0 and code under MIT.

\subsection*{Reproducibility statement}
We will release the generator, training and evaluation code, \texttt{configs/*.yaml}, seeds, an \texttt{environment.yml}, a one-click \texttt{scripts/run.sh}, cached API outputs, LoRA adapters ($<$2\,GB), and both dataset tiers with checksums, together with GPU model, GPU-hours, and peak memory. Compute budget: one NVIDIA A100-40GB or RTX 4090-24GB; $\approx$3 GPU-h per SmolVLM-500M LoRA run (3 epochs, batch 64, bf16, peak memory $<$20\,GB), $\approx$1.5\,h per 256M, $\approx$8\,h per 2.2B; $\approx$45 runs $\approx$160 GPU-h plus $\approx$10 GPU-h of inference; APIs ($\approx$4k images $\times$ 3 models $\times$ 4 prompts; pilot outputs reused) $\approx$ USD~100--150. \textbf{Fine-tuning.} SmolVLM-256M and -500M with LoRA rank 16, $\alpha=32$, dropout 0.05, 3 epochs, batch size 64, learning rate $10^{-4}$ with cosine schedule and 3\% warm-up. Decisions are read by forced-choice scoring over the \textsc{right}/\textsc{wrong} answer tokens rather than free-form generation. The pilot adapters were trained on an earlier 34,018-row release that contained 14 physical-family negatives; all reported metrics are computed on the identical 3,966-row physical-free test set, and the proposed runs will retrain on the physical-free data.

\textbf{Zero-shot protocol.} Each VLM receives the rendered image and a fixed prompt that explains the circle/arrow/caption semantics, states the three validity conditions (correct, consistent, sufficient), and asks for a single-word \textsc{right}/\textsc{wrong} answer; the identical prompt is used for open and closed models.

\textbf{Pilot error profile.} SmolVLM-500M makes 415 test errors: 122 false accepts and 293 false rejects. The weakest slices are arrow-between-destinations (83.1\% correct rejection), wrong destination (88.0\%), wrong magnitude (89.9\%), and near-miss circle (91.7\%)---exactly the geometrically subtle modes, which motivates H3.
